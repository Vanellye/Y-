# Glyph Garden — Creative Seed

## Story — The Shape That Learned to Turn
A circle wanted to describe a journey, not merely a boundary. It learned to lean, to spiral, to pause, and to return. The first symbol was not a final answer; it was a question drawn in motion. Each new glyph kept the gesture that gave it meaning.

## Art direction — Motion glyph sheet
Create a clean black-and-white plate of six original abstract glyphs: swivel (pivot), swurl (curved flow), spiral (returning expansion), swerve (directional deviation), pause (held interval), and recoil (return under tension). Use consistent stroke weight and include a small motion arrow beside each. These are proposed definitions, open to revision.

## Music sketch — “Six Gestures” (original)
Six one-bar cells in 5/4: pivot = D–A–D; flow = D–E–G–A; spiral = D–F–A–C; swerve = D–F♯–C; pause = D (whole-bar sustain); recoil = A–G–E–D. Treat them as composable gestures, not a fixed song.

## Curation rules
Keep each term distinct, version definitions, and record alternate interpretations. A symbol's visual resemblance is not proof of physical or mathematical equivalence.
